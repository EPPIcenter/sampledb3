## What is SampleDB?

SampleDB is a specimen inventory and workflow system for research and clinical labs. Lab staff and biobank operators use it to track biological material from study enrollment through physical storage, including bulk CSV import and export, quality controls, container derivations, and qPCR plate layout.

SampleDB also tracks **quality control specimens**: reference materials produced in defined batches, separate from study subjects. After you register a specimen, the same storage tools apply whether the material came from a study subject or a control batch.

## Core concepts

Most day-to-day work starts with provenance (where the material came from) and then storage (where it is now).

### Provenance

**Studies** are the top-level unit for research projects and clinical trials. Each study has a unique short code (like NAM15 or TCC08) used in imports, exports, and searches.

**Subjects** are participants or sample sources within a study. Each subject has a unique name within that study and can have many specimens.

**Specimens** are the registered record of biological material from a specific collection event. In casual conversation you might say "sample". In SampleDB the domain term is **Specimen**. Each specimen has a type, an optional collection date, and a **source**, typically a subject or a control batch.

Quality control specimens follow a parallel path: **Control definition → Control batch → Specimen**. Strain-mixture controls add a **Composition** layer above definitions. See [Blood Controls](/docs/guides/features/blood-controls/).

### Physical storage

**Containers** hold specimen material: micronix tubes, cryovials, dried blood spot papers, and other types. One specimen can have multiple containers. Each container that holds part of the same specimen is an **aliquot**.

**Collections** group containers handled together as one physical unit: a micronix plate, cryovial box, or bag. A collection is not the act of collecting a sample from a subject.

**Locations** are places in your storage hierarchy where collections sit. Manage them under **Browse Data → Locations**, not in Reference Data. See [Locations](/docs/guides/workflows/locations/).

A specimen can exist without a container until it is stored. When it is stored, the chain is **Specimen → Container → Collection → Location**.

### Processing and configuration

**Derivations** record a transformation from a parent container into a child container, usually with a new specimen type. Aliquoting splits the same specimen across containers without a derivation record. See [Derivations](/docs/guides/features/derivations/).

**Reference data** is system-wide configuration: specimen types, tags, storage types, strains, and units. Studies, specimens, containers, locations, and controls are operational data. See [Reference data](/docs/guides/reference-data/overview/).

## How this guide is organized

- **Getting started.** Setup wizard, dashboard, and a TUT01 walkthrough you can delete.
- **Core workflows.** Studies, subjects, specimens, containers, and locations.
- **Bulk operations.** CSV import, multi-study export, and container moves.
- **Specialized features.** Blood controls, derivations, Move Collections, plate-scan validation, and qPCR experiments.
- **Reference data.** Specimen types, tags, storage types, strains, and units. Locations live under Core workflows.
- **Advanced topics.** Search, statistics, Micronix Barcode Export, and [Application Settings](/docs/guides/advanced/settings/).
- **Troubleshooting.** Import errors, CSV rules, and common issues.
