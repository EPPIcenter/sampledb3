The Statistics page provides comprehensive insights into your SampleDB data, helping you understand storage utilization, specimen distributions, and system-wide metrics. These statistics are valuable for planning, reporting, and understanding how your laboratory's data is organized and growing over time.

Statistics update automatically as data is added to the system, giving you real-time insights into your inventory and operations. Whether you're planning storage expansion, preparing reports for collaborators, or simply understanding your data better, the statistics page provides the information you need.

## Accessing Statistics

Navigate to Statistics in the sidebar to access the comprehensive statistics and reporting interface. The page is organized into sections that cover different aspects of your data, making it easy to find the information you need.

## Understanding System-Wide Metrics

At the top level, you'll see system-wide metrics that give you a high-level view of your entire SampleDB instance. The total studies count shows how many research studies you're managing. The total subjects count shows how many subjects are enrolled across all studies. The total specimens count shows how many biological specimens have been registered. The total containers count shows how many physical containers you're tracking. And the total locations count shows how many storage locations are in your hierarchy.

These metrics provide a quick snapshot of your system's scale and help you understand the overall scope of your data. They're useful for reporting, planning, and getting a sense of how your laboratory's data is growing.

## Storage Statistics and Utilization

Storage statistics help you understand how your samples are distributed across your storage infrastructure. By root location, you can see container counts per location, understand distribution across your storage hierarchy, and review utilization metrics that help identify locations approaching capacity.

By storage type, you can see how containers are organized by storage condition—how many are in -80°C freezers versus -20°C freezers, for example. This temperature-based distribution helps with capacity planning and understanding storage needs. You can also see storage capacity information that helps identify when you might need additional storage.

By collection type, you can see how many Micronix plates, Cryovial boxes, boxes, and bags you have. This breakdown helps you understand how your containers are organized and can inform decisions about collection management and organization.

These storage statistics are particularly valuable for planning. They help you identify locations approaching capacity, plan for additional storage, and reorganize if needed. Understanding where samples are stored and how storage is distributed helps you make informed decisions about storage management.

## Specimen Statistics and Distribution

Specimen statistics show how your biological samples are distributed across different types and studies. By specimen type, you can see counts of each type, distribution percentages that show which types are most common, and trends over time that help you understand how collection patterns are changing.

This information is useful for understanding what types of samples you work with most frequently, planning procurement based on specimen type needs, and tracking collection trends. If you see certain specimen types becoming more or less common, that might inform workflow adjustments or resource planning.

By container type, you can see how many Micronix tubes, Cryovial tubes, papers, and static wells you have. This breakdown helps you understand container preferences and can inform decisions about container procurement and management.

## Study Statistics and Comparison

Study statistics provide insights into individual studies and allow you to compare studies side-by-side. Study summaries show subjects per study, specimens per study, containers per study, and collection date ranges. This helps you understand the scope and progress of each study.

Study comparison features let you compare studies side-by-side, identify your largest studies, and track study progress over time. This is useful for resource allocation, understanding which studies are most active, and planning based on study size and activity.

## Using Statistics for Planning

Storage planning benefits greatly from statistics. Review storage by location to understand distribution, identify locations approaching capacity, plan for additional storage, and reorganize if needed. The utilization metrics help you see which locations are getting full and which have capacity available.

Specimen planning uses distribution statistics to understand what types of samples you work with, track trends in specimen collection, and plan procurement based on specimen type needs. If you see certain types becoming more common, you might need to adjust workflows or procurement.

Study planning uses study statistics to understand study scope, compare study sizes, track study progress, and allocate resources appropriately. Understanding which studies are largest or most active helps with planning and resource management.

## Generating Reports

The statistics page provides the foundation for generating reports. You can use the information displayed to understand your data, and you can export statistics or use them to inform bulk exports. The statistics help you identify what data to include in reports and provide context for understanding exported data.

Some systems may provide export functionality directly from the statistics page, allowing you to export statistical summaries or detailed data based on the statistics you're viewing. This makes it easy to generate reports for collaborators, administrators, or regulatory purposes.

## Best Practices for Using Statistics

Effective use of statistics starts with regular review. Check statistics periodically to understand trends and identify issues early. Use statistics for planning by reviewing utilization metrics before making storage decisions, using distribution statistics to inform procurement, and using study statistics to allocate resources.

Share statistics with your team to keep everyone informed about system status and trends. Use statistics in reports to provide context and demonstrate system usage. And use statistics to identify issues—if utilization metrics show problems or distribution statistics reveal unexpected patterns, investigate and address them.
